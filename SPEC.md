## Judge criteria (in order)

1. A single line that sums up the entrant's own week — the win, the fail
   or the chaos of it.
2. Lands a real joke or a sharp turn of phrase, not merely a feeling.
3. Holds one concrete detail of a week — a thing that happened, a person,
   a place — so the line could only be that week.
4. Spends every word well inside 100 characters, nothing padded, nothing
   missing.
5. Feels fresh, not a saying or a template the reader has met before.

## Why it should win

It packs all three beats of a week into one breath — the win (shipped
three features), the fail (broke prod twice), the chaos — and lands on a
concrete, specific detail (the plants) that makes the line belong to this
week only. The turn is the last clause: everything else died, only the
plants survived. Nothing is padded, nothing is a template ("what a week"
lines will flood the field), and it stays inside 100 characters with room
to spare.
